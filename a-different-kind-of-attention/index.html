Looking closely

A different kind of attention.

Five small things to notice in a glass, none of which require any vocabulary.

Photograph: Thomas Stephan / Unsplash

Wine talk can make people feel they are sitting an exam. You do not need any of it. What follows is a short list of things anyone can notice, slowly, with no right answers.

  1. Where the bubbles start

    Look at the bottom of the glass. The bubbles usually rise from the same few points, in thin, steady threads. Follow one thread from the bottom to the top. It takes longer than you expect.

  2. How they change on the way up

    Each bubble grows a little as it rises. Near the surface they are larger and slower. It is a small, private weather system, and it is completely quiet.

  3. The first sip and the third

    The first sip is mostly cold and bright. By the third, the wine has warmed a degree or two and something rounder appears underneath. Neither one is the real taste. Both are.

  4. What the room does

    Drink the same glass by a window and then in a dim corner. It will seem different. Light, temperature and mood all belong to the experience; there is no neutral setting.

  5. What you keep thinking about

    After the glass is finished, notice what stays with you. A texture, a moment, a sentence someone said. That is the part of the wine that was yours.

None of this needs to be written down or shared. Paying attention is not a performance. It is just a way of being somewhere fully, for the length of one glass.
