Place · The Sonoma vineyard

Stories from Ramal Road.

Our Sonoma vineyard sits on Ramal Road, on the western side of Carneros: about three hundred acres of low hills, wind and fog. Six small things we have learned about the place.

23900 Ramal Road · Sonoma · Los Carneros

I

The rams

Long before vines, this was sheep country. Los Carneros means “the rams”, and the name stuck to these low hills by the bay. The vine came early, too: one of the first vineyards in the area was planted on the Sonoma side in the late 1830s. The sheep moved on. The wind stayed.

II

A station called Branch

Ramal is Spanish for a branch line, and there really was one here: a small country stop on the Southern Pacific, with no stationmaster and no ticket office, just a name on the timetable. By the early 1950s no passengers or freight had come through for two years, and in 1953 the railroad asked to take Ramal off its maps. The trains stopped calling. The road still answers to the old name.

III

From the edge of the bay

In 1878 a railroad was laid from the edge of the bay up to the town of Sonoma, carrying the valley’s harvests toward the wider world. Most of those little lines are memories now, but the track east of Schellville is still in use. Out here, things that work tend to last.

IV

The farmhouse

At the heart of the ranch sits a small farmhouse. Records say it was built in 1924, before the vineyard and long before the wine. It has watched the land change from pasture to rows of vines, one season at a time. We like to think it approves.

V

Water that comes back

Two ponds keep the vines alive through the dry summer. One fills with winter rain running off the hills. The other is filled with recycled water from the local sanitation district, cleaned and given a second life in the vineyard. Nothing grand, just a quiet way of wasting less.

VI

On a clear day

Most mornings the fog sits low over the rows. But on a clear day, from the top of the hill, you can see all the way to the San Francisco skyline. It’s a reminder of how close the city is, and how far away it feels.
